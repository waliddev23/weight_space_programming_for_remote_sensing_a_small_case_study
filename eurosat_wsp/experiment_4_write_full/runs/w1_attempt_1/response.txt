I designed this as 16 hand-made features feeding a nearest-centroid (LDA-style) linear classifier. Nothing was run or tested, and the class statistics behind the weights are my own estimates from memory, so the accuracy is unknown.

Conv filters (designed in pixel units, then converted to the normalised input):

0–2, always-on colour means: brightness, red minus blue, and greenness (G minus the average of R and B). Their biases keep the ReLU open, so they are plain linear averages.
3–7, thresholded colour, centre pixel only: dark pixels (forest, water), bright blue-channel pixels (white roofs), strongly green pixels (pasture), reddish pixels (bare soil), and strongly blue pixels (water).
8–11 and 14–15, edges: Sobel x and y on luminance at three thresholds (0, 1.5 and 5). The classifier subtracts these to get a weak-texture term that saturates, a mid-texture term, and a strong-edge term. The weak term is what separates smooth sea from slightly textured forest, and it is almost immune to the zero-padding border.
12–13, Laplacian: bright and dark spots or thin lines above a threshold (houses, roads, roofs).
Classifier: for each class I estimated the mean of every feature and a shared spread, then set weight = (class mean − overall mean) / variance and the bias so the decision boundary sits midway between centroids. The estimates are anchored to the EuroSAT channel means as I recall them (about 0.344, 0.380, 0.408). A few adjustments were made by hand:

Texture weights are scaled down because those features are strongly correlated.
Residential and Industrial are given equal texture, so they are separated by brightness, white-roof pixels and strong edges.
Sea gets extra weight on smoothness.
Expected border effects from the zero padding are folded into the centroids.
